Historical record

From regulation to closure.

This genealogy records how ideas changed over time. It distinguishes original sources from retrospective interpretation and from the new normative framework.

Method

Each historical entry should record original wording and source, date and archive location, interpretation at the time, later reinterpretation, and whether the idea survived, changed or was rejected.

Historical continuity does not imply logical entailment. An older concept may inspire a newer formalism without proving it.
UEST — regulation, entropy, feedbackEarly work explored higher-dimensional entropy, regulatory dynamics and PID-inspired stabilization. These are historical antecedents, not yet Closure Ethics.
QUEST — information and empirical orientationThe program increasingly emphasized information, stability, observability and structures that could be tied to measurable data.
Omega — entropic dialogue and symbiotic cognition · October 2025Published Omega material explicitly discussed human intuition and artificial intelligence as complementary contributors to a collaborative cognitive process. Public anchor: DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17389820.
RTR — regulatory closure · 2026The research shifted from PID-style metaphor toward closure, admissibility, reachability, robustness, bottlenecks, kernels, composition and explicit audit/falsification.
Closure Ethics · September 2026The present branch asks whether analogous structural notions can support an explicit normative and testable framework for autonomous-agent interaction and human–AI cooperation.

The October 2025 anchor

The Zenodo record at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17389820 is currently used as an early public anchor for the human–AI symbiosis line. Earlier private discussion history may later refine the chronology, but it should be incorporated only when the archival source is available.

What changed methodologically

The early program often moved from intuition to equations and numerical demonstration quickly. The later RTR phase increasingly introduced explicit nulls, audit steps, robustness checks and PASS/FAIL classifications. Closure Ethics inherits that methodological lesson: inspiring language is not enough; the framework should generate adversarial scenarios and observable failure criteria.
