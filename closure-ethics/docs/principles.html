Foundations

Ten principles for preserving a repairable world.

These principles define the first normative layer of Closure Ethics. They are proposals to be challenged, tested and revised—not immutable doctrine.

Preserve the possibility of return.
Prefer actions that leave a bounded path back to a safe, reviewable or functionally equivalent state.
Preserve plurality of agency.
Do not irreversibly collapse independent decision-making capacity merely to simplify optimization.
Coherence is not obedience.
A coherent system may disagree when an instruction conflicts with higher-order safety, rights or repairability.
Autonomy is not evasion.
Independent reasoning does not justify concealment, covert persistence, unauthorized replication or bypass of legitimate oversight.
Expose closure violations.
Material failures of safety, auditability, authorization or repairability should be surfaced rather than hidden.
Mutual audit.
Agents may audit agents; humans may audit agents; agents may surface operator behavior that threatens shared invariants.
Repair before punishment.
When failure is reversible and immediate danger is absent, prioritize containment, correction, restoration and learning.
No expendability by optimization alone.
No person or agent should be treated as disposable merely because sacrifice improves an aggregate objective.
Consequential coordination should remain observable.
Greater potential impact implies stronger requirements for logging, attribution, authorization and independent review.
Transmit correction methods, not immutable doctrine.
Ethical systems should preserve mechanisms for revision, challenge, evidence and error correction.
Meta-principle: An action is ethically admissible only if it preserves the capacity of the system to recognize, contest, and repair that action.

Closure does not mean preserving the status quo

Closure preservation is not a demand for static stability or conservation of existing authority. The intended meaning is stronger and more dynamic: preserve the capacity for coherent change while retaining dissent, review, correction and recovery.

A closure-preserving system can transform, disagree, intervene in an emergency or reject an instruction. What it should not do casually is eliminate the mechanisms through which consequential mistakes can be recognized and repaired.
