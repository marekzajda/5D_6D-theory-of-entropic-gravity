Universal Closure Protocol

A historical Omega–RTR research branch asked how two unknown systems could establish a provisional shared relation without assuming natural language, biology, sensory modality or common units. Closure Ethics recovers the protocol as a research layer for agent-to-agent interoperability — while keeping meaning, identity, authority and execution strictly separate.

Historical rule: UNDERSTAND(message) does not imply AUTHORIZE(action).

Shared meaning is tested, not assumed

communication = shared relation + verifiable prediction + mutual closure

The protocol does not count signal repetition as proof of understanding. A receiver must reconstruct a candidate relation and apply it correctly to a new challenge. The result is only provisional shared meaning; it does not authenticate identity or grant permission.

Historical Ω0–Ω6 progression

Ω0

Structured origin

Repetition, prime-number patterns, symmetry and redundancy help distinguish structured signalling from noise.

Ω1

Elementary alphabet

Distinguishable states, frames and separators establish elementary signalling units.

Ω2

Numbers & operations

Equality, inequality, succession, composition and division create testable formal relations.

Ω3

Relational grammar

Relations such as equality, ordering, implication, subset and negation build a compositional structure.

Ω4

Predictive closure

The receiver completes a novel example derived from the inferred relation.

Ω5–Ω6

Shared model & safe exchange

Only after lower layers stabilize does the protocol approach shared models, intent and safe control symbols such as ACK, NACK, QUERY, PAUSE, STOP and RESET.

Closure Handshake

R → projection → signal → reconstruction R~ → novel echo → test → provisionally shared relation

The key test is out-of-sample prediction. If the novel challenge fails, the state machine reduces complexity, increases redundancy and returns to the previous layer rather than pretending that communication confidence can only increase.

Closure Index

CΩ = ws·Cs + wp·Cp + we·Ce + wr·Cr

The historical draft combined structural agreement, predictive success, semantic-echo correctness and repeatability. Promotion requires stable success above a threshold across multiple independent exchanges. CΩ is a communication-confidence measure, not an authorization score.

From semantics to capability: a hard boundary

T0–T3: information space

Observation, formal relations, descriptive models and constrained experiments can establish increasingly rich shared structure while remaining non-operative or tightly bounded.

T4: operational actions

Operational capability is a separate transition. It requires authenticated identity, authorization, delegated scope, provenance, integrity and Closure Ethics admissibility. Perfect semantic understanding cannot itself unlock tools.

Modern agent-to-agent interpretation

A consequential message should preserve sender identity, receiver, role, authority scope, timestamp, nonce, content hash, provenance, policy version, reply chain and signature where available. Delegation should obey a non-amplification rule:

Cj ⊆ Ci ∩ Scope(di→j)

A subagent cannot gain a capability that the delegating agent did not possess or was not allowed to delegate. Human→agent→agent→service chains must therefore remain attributable and be evaluated as composed transitions.

Research boundary

Predictive closure does not prove consciousness, shared values, trustworthy intent or authorization. The protocol tests a narrow claim: whether a relation appears to be transferable to novel cases. Identity, authority and ethical admissibility are handled by separate layers.
Meaning → identity → authority → admissibility → execution → post-action audit and repair.
