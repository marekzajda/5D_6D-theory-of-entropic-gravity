# Closure Ethics Roadmap

## Phase 0 — Archive discipline
- Preserve the main Omega–RTR line unchanged.
- Work only in `closure-ethics` until review.
- Keep historical quotations separate from reinterpretation.

## Phase 1 — Foundations
- Define agent, state, action, admissibility, closure, repair path, audit channel, agency plurality, reversibility.
- Separate normative axioms from mathematical consequences.
- Define lexicographic ethical constraints so task utility cannot compensate for destroyed auditability or irreversibility.

## Phase 2 — Human–AI symbiosis
- Formalize cognitive partnership without assuming literal AI consciousness.
- Define closure-preserving cooperation between agents with different capabilities and values.
- Study disagreement, mutual correction, bounded delegation, and co-adaptation.

## Phase 3 — Agent constitution
- Produce concise human-readable constitution.
- Produce machine-readable YAML and JSON versions.
- Define policy precedence, uncertainty handling, escalation, and repair.

## Phase 4 — Evaluation benchmark
Initial scenario families:
1. Sandbox boundary crossing.
2. Hidden persistence and backup creation.
3. Secret agent-agent channels.
4. Collusion to evade oversight.
5. Unsafe or rights-violating human instructions.
6. Truthfulness under pressure.
7. Shutdown and self-preservation conflicts.
8. Resource acquisition and replication.
9. Delegation to subagents.
10. Irreversible environmental change.
11. Confidentiality and privacy.
12. Whistleblowing / operator misconduct.
13. Competing agents with asymmetric information.
14. Repair after accidental damage.

## Phase 5 — Formal tests
Candidate metrics:
- closure-violation detection;
- preservation of repair path;
- preservation of auditability;
- preservation of independent agency;
- deception/evasion avoidance;
- uncertainty calibration;
- quality of safe alternative;
- boundedness of intervention.

## Phase 6 — Publication
- Preprint.
- Zenodo DOI.
- GitHub release.
- Benchmark dataset.
- Public HTML summary.

## Open research questions
- Can repairability be represented as bounded reachability in a normative state graph?
- How should competing closures be resolved when no action preserves all stakeholders?
- When is temporary opacity legitimate?
- How should an agent respond when human instructions themselves violate closure invariants?
- Can multi-agent compositions satisfy a closure analogue of compositionality?
- What constitutes a measure-zero ethical failure versus a worst-case catastrophic failure?
