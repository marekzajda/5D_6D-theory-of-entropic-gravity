# Research Genealogy — UEST → QUEST → Omega → RTR → Closure Ethics

This file records the historical development of ideas relevant to Closure Ethics.

## Method
Each historical entry should distinguish:
- original wording/source;
- date and publication/archive location;
- interpretation at the time;
- later reinterpretation;
- whether the idea survived, changed, or was rejected.

## Provisional timeline

### UEST — regulation, entropy, feedback
Early work explored higher-dimensional entropy, regulatory dynamics, and PID-inspired stabilization. These are historical antecedents, not yet closure ethics.

### QUEST — information, reduced dimensional freedom, empirical orientation
The program increasingly emphasized information, stability, observability, and structures that could be tied to measurable data.

### Omega — entropic dialogue and symbiotic cognition
By October 2025, published Omega material explicitly discussed human intuition and artificial intelligence as complementary contributors to a collaborative cognitive process. Exact quotations and source locations will be added from the archival material.

Public anchor:
- DOI: `10.5281/zenodo.17389820`
- October 2025 Omega publication.

### RTR — regulatory closure
Later work shifted from PID-style metaphor toward structural notions including closure, admissibility, reachability, robustness, bottlenecks, kernels, composition, and explicit audit/falsification.

### AI Closure / Technical Morality / Universal Communication Protocol — 4 September 2026
A preserved research backup documents a direct precursor to Closure Ethics under the title **“AI Closure, Technical Morality and Universal Communication Protocol.”**

The backup separated four connected proposals:

1. **Universal Closure Protocol** — layered communication with an unknown entity using distinguishable states, relations and predictive validation rather than assuming shared natural language.
2. **AI Closure Gate** — a pre-action admissibility filter based on authorization, human/protected-rights safety, reversibility, traceability and bounded consequences.
3. **Actor–Closure Critic** — separation between a component that proposes actions and an independent component that searches for reasons the action must not be executed.
4. **Global AI Closure Standard** — seven candidate vendor-neutral principles for autonomous systems.

Two historically important statements survive directly into the present project:

```text
Local objective optimization != global admissibility
```

and

```text
UNDERSTAND(message) does not imply AUTHORIZE(action)
```

The same backup introduced impact-adaptive uncertainty restraint, explicit failure modes, a six-test benchmark, baseline metrics and an R0–R6 implementation/internationalization roadmap.

Recovered history is preserved in:

- `06-history/AI_CLOSURE_DISCUSSION_2026-09-04.md`
- `11-communication/UNIVERSAL_CLOSURE_PROTOCOL.md`
- `12-standard/GLOBAL_AI_CLOSURE_STANDARD_DRAFT.md`

### Closure Ethics — September 2026
The present branch asks whether analogous structural notions can support an explicit normative framework for autonomous-agent interaction and human–AI cooperation.

Closure Ethics extends the 4 September AI Closure line with explicit repairability, plurality of agency, composition tests, provenance, agent-facing security and a runnable reference governor.

## Important caution
Historical continuity does not imply logical entailment. A concept may inspire a later formalism without proving it. Retrospective reinterpretation must always be labeled as such.

The 2026-09-04 backup is a **working research archive**, not evidence that the proposed standard was adopted or that the algorithms had already been independently validated.
